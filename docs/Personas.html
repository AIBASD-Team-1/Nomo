Nomo Personas (Team 1)

Proto-personas based on our product proposal, web standards (W3C) and persona guides (NN/g, Digital NSW). Each lists the assumptions we still need to validate.

1. Faisal Al-Harbi, product engineer, Riyadh

Background: 34, mechanical engineer at a furniture manufacturer. Arabic is his first language; he reads English technical terms but writes ideas in Arabic.

Goals and motivations: a first 3D draft of a customer request within minutes, to show colleagues before detailed CAD work.

Behaviour and frustrations: writes metric dimensions, mixing Arabic-Indic and Western digits. Dislikes English-only tools, broken right-to-left layouts and machine-sounding translations.

Context: office PC (Windows, Edge), high CAD skills, locale ar-SA, expects formal Modern Standard Arabic. No specific accessibility needs known.

Scenario: he switches Nomo to Arabic, describes a 100 mm box in Arabic, and gets an Arabic reply and the parameter table.

Implications: language switch, RTL layout, Arabic input with both digit styles and Arabic messages are the first demo stories.

Evidence / to validate: W3C RTL guidance. Assumed: digit mixing and preference for Arabic input; digit use and wording checked by a native speaker before 27 November.

2. Omar Al-Qahtani, design student with red-green colour blindness, Jeddah

Background: 19, first-year industrial design student with deuteranopia; uses Arabic and English.

Goals and motivations: simple 3D models for assignments and 3D printing without learning full CAD, and without asking others what a colour means.

Behaviour and frustrations: starts from example prompts, uses dark mode, relies on icons and text. Red/green status colours and colour-only tab markers fail for him.

Context: own laptop, good digital skills, CAD beginner. Locale ar-SA; Arabic UI with familiar, neutral icons. Needs icon + text for status, a blue/orange palette and good contrast.

Scenario: he sends an empty prompt, sees a warning icon with an Arabic message, then turns on the colour-blind setting.

Implications: colour-blind setting is priority 2; no new UI may use colour as the only signal (WCAG 1.4.1).

Evidence / to validate: W3C Use of Color. Assumed: dark mode use and that blue/orange works for him; palette tested with a simulator and, if possible, a colour-blind student.

3. Lukas Hofer, intralogistics engineer, Graz

Background: 38, design engineer at an intralogistics company that builds shelving, storage and conveyor systems for warehouses. German speaker, works in English with international customers.

Goals and motivations: quick, dimension-exact STEP drafts of standard parts (shelf bays, bins, brackets) for early customer layouts and quotes, based on company templates.

Behaviour and frustrations: short English prompts with exact sizes, then a correction and STEP download. Dislikes AI that ignores dimensions or invents non-standard geometry, and updates that change his workflow.

Context: office workstation with two screens, high CAD skills, locale de-AT, expects metric units and European standard sizes. No specific accessibility needs known.

Scenario: he enters "Shelf bay 1200 x 800 x 2000 mm, five levels", checks the values against the company standard and downloads STEP for the layout.

Implications: English stays default and must keep working (existing tests); company templates, rule violations and STEP download stay in the backlog.

Evidence / to validate: our proposal and spec. Assumed: STEP and company templates are his main needs; template and STEP needs to be checked with an engineer at a Styrian intralogistics company.

Sources: W3C qa-html-dir; W3C WCAG 1.4.1 Use of Color; NN/g Personas Study Guide; Digital NSW persona guide.
